## Core principle

A landlord should not treat the dwelling as recovered until the tenancy has actually ended and possession has been lawfully returned.

A Notice of Termination does not itself give a landlord a licence to enter, clear, occupy or take control of the dwelling before the tenancy ends.

## Do not enter early

If a tenancy ends at a stated date and time, the practical rule for a landlord is simple: **do not enter before that point unless the tenant has clearly surrendered possession earlier or another lawful basis for entry applies.**

Do not assume that the tenant has left merely because the property appears quiet, belongings seem to have been removed, keys are expected, or the final day has arrived.

Where there is any uncertainty, communicate first and avoid creating a dispute that did not need to exist.

## Remember the tenant's position

Moving out can be stressful, disruptive and emotionally difficult. A landlord should allow the tenant the full period to which they are entitled and avoid unnecessary pressure during the final hours or days of the tenancy.

The fact that a landlord wants access quickly does not reduce the tenant's rights.

## Lawful access before the tenancy ends

A landlord may have rights of reasonable access for purposes such as inspection or repairs, but those rights are not the same as repossession. Appropriate notice, agreement and the statutory context still matter.

Emergency access is a separate issue and should not be confused with ordinary recovery of possession.

## Keys and surrender

Returning keys is strong practical evidence that possession has been surrendered, but the legal position depends on the facts. Where possible, record the handover clearly in writing.

Useful records can include:

- date and time of key return
- confirmation that the tenant has vacated
- meter readings
- photographs taken after possession has been returned
- any agreed arrangements for belongings left behind

## After possession is returned

Once lawful possession has been recovered, the landlord should document the condition of the dwelling and deal carefully with any remaining belongings, deposit issues, repairs and reletting obligations.

## Disputes about possession

If a tenant remains in occupation after a tenancy is said to have ended, a landlord should use the lawful RTB and court processes available rather than attempting self-help eviction.

Changing locks, removing belongings, cutting utilities, intimidation or physically forcing a tenant out can create serious legal consequences.

## 2026 tenancy-law context

Irish residential-tenancy rules changed from **1 March 2026**. The legal grounds on which a landlord may end a tenancy can depend on when the tenancy began and, for newer tenancies, the landlord category. Always check the current RTB guidance and the Residential Tenancies Act 2004 as amended.
